The Ocean Tried to Kill Me

I feel terror. I feel terror as I fight a magnificent wave with everything I can muster. My arms are flailing in the brackish water in my mouth, my nose, my panicked brain. My body can feel the wave beckoning to join the ocean. My mind and body are resisting the unassailable wave. Then, this wave is superposed by another cajoling wave.

My mind, familiar and functional with panic, anxiety, and stress, currently feels exposed. I feel like a friend who knows me well is excoriating a raw and unhealed wound. I don't feel the pain that accompanies this betrayal, I feel the anguish that accompanies the realization that a loved one is capable of wielding this weapon. Does this mean that I considered water a friend? I did. Minutes ago, the water was peaceful. It struck the rocks on which I sat. It beckoned the rock to slowly, surely dissolve in its magnanimous cajoling being. Its rhythm in the dim moonlight and twinkle of a sleepy city coaxed me to be carefree.

Now, the water is a passionate, affectionate, and devoted lover. Slowly, it chips away at my defenses, erodes me every time I choose to believe in its love. I made space for love by giving a part of myself away. I remember dismissing the thought that I am being robbed. The ocean and my mind are robbing me without my consent. How can a mind rob its home and think it is not consensual? This contradiction is the delusional love. Bit by bit, my defenses are lowered. I now want to be a part of the ocean. I want to experience life as the ocean, my love. My mind, with all its resilience, now believes that the ocean has so much to offer me. The fallacy of it all stirs up in me, and my love mirrors the chaotic, rhythmic thoughts in me, gently coaxing me to surrender, submit, and submerge. I think that I've always been a part of it. We will create our world together. Like a fool falling for a fallacy, I fondle the water in futile resignation, hoping that it will fulfill me, as I fulfilled it. A fulfilled fragment of my imagination then topples. It topples to reveal the malice inside my lover's lovely faculties. I dismiss the malice, misery, moronic misgivings. I poured more into it, reveling in our victory. I kept pouring until it turned out to be bottomless and engulfed all of me. I was empty, barely floating, fragments glued by resilience dismissed by my mind.

And I learned that every fight need not be alone.

Hands I don't recognize, rough and certain, haul me from the ocean that promised. The ocean screams - or is that me? We are separating, cells tearing from cells, and the pain is exquisite, surgical. I am breathing in air that tastes like metal and shame. On the rocks, again. Now they are accusatory, witness to my stupidity. The moon has turned its face away. The city lights blink: fool, fool, fool. My lungs are coughing up saltwater truths. Each heave empties me further - not of water now, but of the fantasy that could never be fulfilled. I can see my mind crouched beside me. Mortal. Breakable. Concerned. It did not save me alone because it could not. This is the lesson the ocean taught in its benevolent violence: that my wall, my fortress, my impenetrable self was porous, permeable, and pedestrian. The cracks were there before the water found them.

I look back at the waves. Rhythmic, and wearing everything down to nothing. The ocean has already forgotten me. I was just another edge to erase. The terror remains. Now, it has a different taste. Not the terror of drowning, the terror of surviving. The terror of being rescued against your will, of having to walk away from the thing that promised to complete you by consuming you. The terror of learning that love is not the ocean. That I am not meant to dissolve.

Fragments, yes. Glued by resilience, yes. But, standing. He who pulled me back - remains, solid as the rocks, refusing to let me mythologize my own erasure.

Every fight need not be alone.
